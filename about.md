I am not a young man. I grew up before the internet existed, at least as we know it. When personal computers came along they did not interest me. In high school I began an elective course that assigned an Apple IIe, and a floppy disc that was actually floppy, but I quickly changed to wood shop.

My first computer, a hand-me-down Toshiba laptop, came years after most folks were familiar with computers through work and/or owning their own. But I had no use for it in my life, until I installed a AOL from a disc given for free at the supermarket.

I'll get back to computers.

## Blue Collared Chef's Coat

When I turned 15 years old my father signed a California work permit so that I could get a job at Burger King. There I realized that I am naturally a good, hard worker. I stayed with the job for two and a half years.

At 18, while still in school, I got a job cooking in a Mexican/Tex-Mex restaurant. From there I ran the kitchen at an old-school American steak house, serving prime rib and lobster. My full-time cooking career concluded as sous to a French chef whose work was respected in San Francisco Bay area restaurant circles.

I had held management positions and keys to several well-established restaurants by age 24 but the restaurant business, if done well, is a lifestyle rather than a career, and I needed a change of lifestyle.

## Digging for Something Else

Given the opportunity to attend college I moved to Southern California to study horticulture at UCLA. Thereafter I worked at four popular nurseries from West Los Angeles to Pasadena.

I was to tolerate the mundanity of sales work as a learning experience, gaining knowledge of plant varieties that can't be learned in a college certificate program, before launching a garden design business.

A few garden design jobs found their way to me but folks simply wanted installation of their own purchases and I was not in a position to procure more than one truck and the tools needed to compete. So again I needed a change.

## Dragging the Line

I often hiked the canyons north of Pasadena, exploring the ruins of a once "great hiking era" left after recurring fires and floods. Then I went a bit further east and discovered a canyon community that has survived, with over 100 historic cabins and a group resort, all beyond the end of the road. I discovered that they are connected by crank telephone and supplied by a working pack station.

So knowing nothing more about equine than how to fall off of a horse, I was hired as the pack station's ranch hand and moved into the bunk house. They showed me the ropes, quite literally, and the shovel.

As fun and fascinating and unique as this mountain adventure was, the fact remained that I had spent five years there and I was now over 40 years old, living in a barn.

## The Code to Getting Out

The pack station was only 45 minutes from the world's entertainment capital but could not get television. However we did wave the internet. So instead of watching other people do things I began looking under the hoods of websites to decipher how they work.

Armed with yet another hand-me-down computer, this time with an old but not entirely obsolete web page builder, I began to figure it out. While living in that bunk house I built my first website, a site for the pack station. It was antiquated before I published it but I was learning.

To reward you for reading this far I will spare you the timeline of my web code knowledge. Suffice it to say that we are here now.
